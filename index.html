The Beauty of Numbers

A simple pattern connecting consecutive numbers and their squares

Mathematical Identity

My Formula

x + (x + 1) = (x + 1)² − x²

The sum of two consecutive numbers equals the difference between their squares.

How does it work?

1. Start with the right side:

(x + 1)² − x²

2. Expand:

x² + 2x + 1 − x²

3. Cancel x²:

2x + 1

4. Now the left side:

x + (x + 1) = 2x + 1

Therefore, both sides are equal. ✓

Example: x = 5

5 + 6 = 11

6² − 5² = 36 − 25 = 11

Therefore: 5 + 6 = 6² − 5² = 11 ✓

Why this pattern is interesting

It connects three ideas in one line: consecutive numbers, their sum, and the difference of their squares.

x + (x + 1) = 2x + 1 = (x + 1)² − x²

Try it yourself

Choose any number x. Calculate x + (x + 1), then calculate (x + 1)² − x². The answers will match.
