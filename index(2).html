1 Consecutive Numbers & Squares

x + (x + 1) = (x + 1)² − x²

Proof

Start with the right-hand side:

(x + 1)² − x²

Expand the square:

x² + 2x + 1 − x² = 2x + 1

Now simplify the left-hand side:

x + (x + 1) = 2x + 1

Therefore, both sides are equal:

x + (x + 1) = (x + 1)² − x² ✓

Example: x = 5

Left side:
5 + 6 = 11

Right side:
6² − 5² = 36 − 25 = 11

Therefore: 11 = 11 ✓
Pattern: The difference between the squares of two consecutive numbers equals their sum.

2 Consecutive Cubes

[(x + 1)³ − x³] − [x³ − (x − 1)³] = 6x

Proof

First simplify the first bracket:

(x + 1)³ − x³
= x³ + 3x² + 3x + 1 − x³
= 3x² + 3x + 1

Now simplify the second bracket:

x³ − (x − 1)³
= x³ − (x³ − 3x² + 3x − 1)
= 3x² − 3x + 1

Subtract the two results:

(3x² + 3x + 1) − (3x² − 3x + 1)
= 6x ✓

Shorter Form

(x + 1)³ − 2x³ + (x − 1)³ = 6x

Example: x = 5

(6³ − 5³) − (5³ − 4³)
= (216 − 125) − (125 − 64)
= 91 − 61
= 30

6 × 5 = 30

Both sides are equal. ✓

Example: x = 10

(11³ − 10³) − (10³ − 9³)
= (1331 − 1000) − (1000 − 729)
= 331 − 271
= 60

6 × 10 = 60

Verified. ✓
Pattern: This is a finite-difference identity for the cubic function. The second difference of consecutive cubes produces the linear expression 6x.
